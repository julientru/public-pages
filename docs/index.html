About this prototype

Prototype dashboard · trend recommendations and growth forecasts built from real Yahoo Finance historical data across major global markets.

Not real financial advice. This is a UX prototype. Recommendations are generated by a simple trend-following rule (log-price regression + recent momentum), and forecasts are linear extrapolations of past trends — not predictions. Past performance does not indicate future results.

Forecasting models

Every asset can be analyzed with any of these eight independent models plus an Ensemble that blends them — pick one on the Dashboard's "Forecast models" section to overlay it on the chart, or rank the whole universe by it on the Rankings tab. All of them work on log-price (ln of the close), so a straight line in the model means constant percentage growth, not constant dollar growth.

Linear Trend

The original model. Fits a linear regression to log-price over the selected lookback and projects it forward — equivalent to extrapolating the average compound growth rate. Also drives the Bullish/Bearish/Neutral recommendation and the composite ranking score, by comparing this long-term trend against recent momentum.

yᵢ = ln(priceᵢ), xᵢ = days since first bar b = Σ(xᵢ-x̄)(yᵢ-ȳ) / Σ(xᵢ-x̄)² slope, ln($)/day a = ȳ - b·x̄ intercept R² = 1 - Σ(yᵢ-ŷᵢ)² / Σ(yᵢ-ȳ)² fit quality, 0-1 forecast(x) = exp(a + b·x)

Forecast horizon = max(30, 25% of the lookback's span in days). Annualized growth = exp(b·365.25) − 1.

Regression line fit through ln(price) history (dots), then extrapolated (dashed) — the slope is the model's forecast.

Moving Average Crossover

Compares a short (20-bar) and long (50-bar) simple moving average of log-price. The forecast extrapolates the short average's recent slope — smoother and less sensitive to single-day outliers than the raw linear-trend fit.

SMAw(i) = mean of ln(price) over the trailing w bars ending at i lookback = min(20, n-1) slope = ( SMA₂₀(last) − SMA₂₀(last − lookback) ) / lookback forecast(h) = exp( SMA₂₀(last) + slope · h )

"Golden cross" when SMA₂₀ ≥ SMA₅₀ at the last bar, otherwise "death cross" — reported as context, not used in the forecast math itself. Needs at least 55 bars (longWindow + 5).

Short SMA's recent slope (dashed) is extrapolated forward; the long SMA is shown only to read the crossover.

ARIMA(1,1,0)

A simplified ARIMA: the log-price series is differenced once, then a first-order autoregression is fit on those differences (no moving-average term, no automatic order selection). A full ARIMA fit needs numerical optimization that's out of scope for a client-side prototype — this is a deliberate, labeled simplification of the standard method.

dᵢ = ln(priceᵢ) − ln(priceᵢ₋₁) "I": difference once dₜ = φ·dₜ₋₁ + c + εₜ "AR": fit by OLS on (dₜ₋₁, dₜ) pairs φ = Σ(x-x̄)(y-ȳ)/Σ(x-x̄)², clamped to [-0.98, 0.98] c = ȳ - φ·x̄ long-run daily drift = c / (1 - φ)

Forecast iterates d̂ₜ₊₁ = φ·dₜ + c one day at a time and accumulates it onto ln(price); needs ≥30 bars. No MA(q) term at all — that's the honest gap vs. a real ARIMA fit.

Worked example If φ = 0.15 and the most recent daily log-return is d = 0.004 (~+0.4%), with c = 0.0003:
next day's diff ≈ 0.15 × 0.004 + 0.0003 = 0.00090 → price drifts up ~0.09% that day, then the projection repeats using its own output as the new "previous diff", decaying toward the long-run drift c/(1-φ) as φᵏ shrinks.
A stable φ (|φ| < 1) means each successive day's AR contribution decays — the forecast settles near the long-run drift.

Holt's Linear Trend

Double exponential smoothing on log-price (a level component and a trend component, no seasonality — irregular trading calendars don't have a clean period to model). The trend is damped (multiplied by a factor below 1 at each step forward) — without damping, a locally strong trend compounds linearly forever and produces wildly overconfident long-horizon forecasts.

levelᵢ = α·yᵢ + (1-α)·(levelᵢ₋₁ + φ·trendᵢ₋₁) trendᵢ = β·(levelᵢ - levelᵢ₋₁) + (1-β)·φ·trendᵢ₋₁ α = 0.3 β = 0.1 φ = 0.98 (fixed, not fit) h-step forecast: level + trend·(φ¹+φ²+…+φʰ) = level + trend· φ(1-φʰ)/(1-φ)

The geometric-series closed form lets the model handle fractional h (sub-day chart steps) without a loop. Needs ≥10 bars.

Each future step's trend contribution is multiplied by another φ (0.98) — bars shrink toward zero instead of adding a constant trend forever.

GARCH(1,1)

Forecasts volatility, not price direction. Rather than adding its own line to the chart, its volatility estimate sets the width of the Ensemble's uncertainty band, and is shown as a standalone "current annualized volatility" figure.

rₜ = ln(priceₜ/priceₜ₋₁), r̄ = mean(r), uncond.Var = mean((rₜ-r̄)²) α = 0.08 β = 0.90 (fixed, not MLE-fit) ω = uncond.Var · (1 - α - β) σ²ₜ = ω + α·(rₜ₋₁-r̄)² + β·σ²ₜ₋₁ recursion over the whole history σ²(h) = uncond.Var + (σ²ₙ - uncond.Var) · (α+β)ʰ h-day-ahead forecast

Daily vol = √σ²ₙ; annualized vol = daily vol × √252. Persistence α+β = 0.98 < 1, so forecast variance mean-reverts toward the unconditional variance as h grows — never explodes or flatlines instantly. Needs ≥30 daily returns.

An elevated current variance decays geometrically (rate α+β) back toward the long-run average — mean reversion, not a straight line.

Feature Regression (ML-style)

A small linear regression predicting next-day return from engineered features — the prior day's return, the gap between a 5-day and 20-day moving average, and a 14-day RSI-like momentum measure — then projected forward recursively, day by day. A genuine feature-engineering + linear-model approach computed fresh in-browser; not a stand-in for a trained Random Forest, LSTM, or Transformer, which don't fit a static client-side prototype.

features = [ 1, lagReturnᵢ, (SMA₅-SMA₂₀)ᵢ, rsiᵢ - 0.5 ] r̂ᵢ = β₀ + β₁·lagReturn + β₂·maGap + β₃·(rsi-0.5) rsi(i) = gains / (gains + losses) over trailing 14 signed daily returns, 0..1 β solved via OLS normal equations: (XᵀX)β = Xᵀy

Each day's predicted return is clamped to ±15%, then fed back in as the next day's "lag return" — a recursive, AR-like walk using the fitted features instead of the raw series. Needs ≥40 bars, ≥15 training rows.

Each predicted return becomes next step's "lagReturn" feature — a recursive one-step-ahead loop, not a single multi-day fit.

Sentiment Proxy (simulated)

No real news or text data source is connected. This score is derived purely from a shift in recent price momentum, framed the way a sentiment score would read. It exists to show where a real news/NLP signal would plug into the Ensemble — not as a genuine sentiment measure.

recentMean = mean(last 20 daily log-returns) olderMean = mean(the 20 daily log-returns before that) shift = recentMean - olderMean sentiment = clamp(shift / 0.01, -1, +1) scale=0.01 tunes typical shifts to ±1 impliedDailyDrift = sentiment · recentMean · 0.5

Label: sentiment > 0.3 → "Positive", < -0.3 → "Negative", else "Neutral". The ×0.5 factor deliberately keeps this a modest tilt on top of recent momentum, not a dominant signal — honestly, there is little real math here to be rigorous about, since the underlying signal is a stand-in for data this prototype doesn't have.

Monte Carlo Simulation

Simulates hundreds of random price paths under geometric Brownian motion, using the asset's historical drift and GARCH-estimated volatility, then reports the 10th-90th percentile of simulated outcomes — a distribution of possible futures instead of one line.

ln(priceₜ₊₁) = ln(priceₜ) + μ + σ·Z, Z ~ N(0,1) μ = mean historical daily log-return σ = GARCH's dailyVol (or historical std-dev if GARCH unavailable) Z drawn via Box-Muller from a seeded PRNG (seed = round(lastPrice·1000), reproducible per run) 500 paths simulated → sort final prices → report P10 / P50 / P90

Rankings uses a lighter 80-path run without keeping per-path chart data, for speed across the whole asset universe. Needs ≥20 bars.

A handful of the simulated paths (grey) fan out from today's price; the shaded band spans the 10th-90th percentile of all simulated endpoints.

How Rankings works

The Rankings tab scores every asset in the selected filter (class, country) and lists the top or bottom 1/5/10/25/100 by whichever metric is chosen under "Rank by":

  • Composite score (default) — blends 60% long-term trend growth with 40% recent momentum, then discounts that blend for low-confidence fits (a noisy R²) and for volatility above 20%/yr — so a noisy short-term spike can't outrank a steady, well-supported trend.
  • Any individual model — ranks assets directly by that model's forecast % at the selected lookback. Assets are skipped where the model needs more history than is available at that lookback (e.g. ARIMA, Moving Average Crossover, and Feature Regression all need 30-50+ bars of data).
rankScore = (0.6·longTermGrowth + 0.4·momentumGrowth) · confidenceFactor · volPenalty confidenceFactor = 0.4 + 0.6·clamp(R², 0, 1) range 0.4-1.0 volPenalty = 1 / (1 + max(0, annualVol - 0.20)) penalizes vol above 20%/yr

Use Top to see the highest-scoring assets, or Bottom to see the lowest-scoring ones — useful for spotting names the model is bearish on, not only bullish on. Clicking any row expands its full Dashboard-equivalent detail — chart, recommendation, stats, forecast models, backtest — inline, without leaving the Rankings tab.

How the Dividends tab works

A separate ranking, built on different data than everything else in this app: real historical dividend payments, annual and quarterly financial statements (free cash flow, revenue), and analyst EPS estimates for ~900 large-cap companies across all 20 countries in the asset universe (top ~50 per country, fewer for smaller markets that don't have 50 real liquid large-caps) — not price history, and not the full ~940-asset universe.

Every company can be scored by any of 9 independent models, selectable under "Rank by" — matching how the Dashboard offers several price-forecast models rather than one blended number. Click into any company to see all 9 compared side by side.

Composite

The original model: 40% dividend growth consistency (from real payment history, penalized for any cut >10% year-over-year) + 40% sustainability (60% earnings-based payout ratio + 40% the level of FCF margin — not just its trend, so a company stuck at a deeply negative margin can't hide behind a decent payout ratio — then nudged by FCF margin trend and recent quarterly FCF momentum) + 20% current yield.

growthScore: ≥2 cuts → 5 1 cut → 30 else clamp(50 + CAGR·400, 0, 100) payoutTierScore: comfortable 90 · moderate 65 · tight 35 · exceeds earnings 10 · unknown 50 fcfMarginLevelScore = clamp(50 + latestFcfMargin·150, 0, 100) sustainScore = payoutTierScore·0.6 + fcfMarginLevelScore·0.4 + clamp(marginTrend·40, -15, +15) trend nudge, capped + clamp(qoqFcfGrowth·40, -10, +10) momentum nudge, capped yieldScore = clamp(yieldPct · 1200, 0, 100) e.g. 8.3% yield → 100 Composite = growthScore·0.4 + sustainScore·0.4 + yieldScore·0.2
Worked example 5%/yr dividend CAGR (0.05), no cuts, payout ratio 45% ("comfortable"), FCF margin 0.12 (12%), flat trend, yield 0.03 (3.0%):
growthScore = clamp(50+0.05·400, 0,100) = 70 · fcfMarginLevelScore = clamp(50+0.12·150,0,100) = 68 · sustainScore = 90·0.6+68·0.4 = 81.2 · yieldScore = clamp(0.03·1200,0,100) = 36
Composite = 70·0.4 + 81.2·0.4 + 36·0.2 = 67.7 → Stable (50-70 tier).
The three weighted components (40/40/20) stack into the final Composite score.

Tenure (streak)

How many consecutive years has this company avoided a cut, from real payment history? A 15-year unbroken streak scores at the top of the scale — distinct from the Composite's CAGR, since the same growth rate over 3 years vs. 15 years is a very different track record.

score = clamp( (consecutiveYearsNoCut / 15) · 100, 0, 100 )

A "cut" is any year-over-year drop >10% in the annual dividend total (see the shared cut-detection logic below). The first and last calendar years on record are dropped before counting — they're usually partial years from the fetch window, which would misread as a cut.

Score rises linearly with streak length and caps at 100 once the streak reaches 15 years.

FCF Payout Ratio

Recomputes the payout ratio directly from real numbers — total dividends paid (estimated from per-share amounts × shares outstanding) ÷ free cash flow — rather than using the earnings-based ratio the Composite relies on. Some companies look sustainable on earnings but are straining cash flow, or the reverse; this can disagree with the Composite when that happens. A year where FCF was negative while a dividend was paid scores at the bottom of the scale, rather than being excluded from the ranking.

sharesOutstanding ≈ marketCap / price totalDividendsPaid = (per-share annual total for the FCF year) · sharesOutstanding fcfPayoutRatio = totalDividendsPaid / freeCashFlow

Volatility-Adjusted Yield

Current yield divided by the stock's own annualized price volatility (last 24 months, the same measure used in the Dashboard's forecast models). A 4% yield from a steady utility scores higher than the same 4% from a volatile miner.

ratio = yieldPct / annualVolatility(last 24 months) score = clamp(ratio · 200, 0, 100) ratio ≈ 0.5 (e.g. 5% yield / 10% vol) tops the scale
Worked example Utility: 4% yield, 12% annual volatility → ratio = 0.333 → score = clamp(66.7, 0,100) = 66.7.
Miner: same 4% yield, 45% annual volatility → ratio = 0.089 → score = 17.8. Identical yield, very different score.

Leverage-Adjusted

Blends debt-to-equity (60%) with the payout-ratio tier (40%) — high leverage can make an otherwise-comfortable payout fragile. Not computed for Financial Services companies (banks, insurers), who don't report debt-to-equity the way industrials do — shown as not applicable rather than guessed.

Sector-Relative Yield

Ranks a company's yield as a percentile against other companies in the same sector within this ~900-company dividend dataset (not the whole market) — a 3% yield is mediocre for a bank but strong for a tech company, so this reframes yield by what's typical for that sector here.

percentile = (# sector peers with lower yield) / (# sector peers) score = clamp(percentile · 100, 0, 100)

Needs ≥3 sector peers with known yield in the dataset, otherwise scored as insufficient data.

6 of 8 peers yield less → 6/8 = 75th percentile → score 75.

Analyst-Implied Forward (derived, not a real analyst call)

Yahoo has no direct analyst dividend forecast field — only forward EPS estimates. This projects next year's dividend as (forward EPS estimate × current payout ratio), assuming the payout ratio holds steady, and nudges the result by the recent balance of analyst EPS estimate revisions (more raising vs. cutting in the last 30 days). Labeled as a derived projection, not a genuine analyst dividend call.

impliedForwardDividend = forwardEpsNextYear · payoutRatio impliedGrowthPct = (impliedForwardDividend / currentDividendRate - 1) · 100 score = clamp(50 + impliedGrowthPct·5, 0, 100) revisionBalance = (upRevisions - downRevisions) / (upRevisions + downRevisions) score = clamp(score + revisionBalance·10, 0, 100) ±10 pt nudge, only if any revisions exist
Worked example Current dividend rate $2.00/share, forward EPS $5.50, payout ratio 40% → implied forward dividend = $2.20 → impliedGrowthPct = +10%.
score = clamp(50 + 10·5, 0,100) = 100. With 2 analysts raising, 1 cutting estimates: revisionBalance = 1/3 → score = clamp(100+3.3,0,100) = 100 (already capped).

Growth Deceleration

A single CAGR over the whole dividend history can't tell a company that grew fast early and has since slowed down apart from one that's held a steady pace throughout. This compares dividend growth in the earliest third of the years on record to the most recent third — catching a slowdown a long-run average would smooth over. Needs at least 6 years of dividend history to compute.

segmentSize = max(2, floor(years.length / 3)) earlyCagr = CAGR of the first `segmentSize` years on record recentCagr = CAGR of the last `segmentSize` years on record decelerationPts = (earlyCagr - recentCagr) · 100 percentage points score = clamp(80 - decelerationPts · 4, 0, 100)
decelerationPtsTrend label
≤ -1accelerating
-1 to 1holding steady
1 to 5decelerating
> 5sharply decelerating
Worked example Early-years CAGR 12%/yr, recent-years CAGR 4%/yr → decelerationPts = (12-4) = 8 → score = clamp(80 - 8·4, 0,100) = 48 ("sharply decelerating", Caution tier).
Two separate CAGR line segments (earliest third vs. most recent third of years) — the gap between their steepness is the deceleration.

Earnings Quality

Compares net income growth to free cash flow growth over the same annual window, both from real financial statements. A company can report growing earnings while cash flow doesn't keep pace (working capital changes, capex timing, one-off gains) — that's a subtler, earlier warning sign than the payout ratio or FCF margin alone catch, since it questions whether the earnings behind the payout ratio are backed by real cash.

netIncomeCagr = (lastNetIncome / firstNetIncome)^(1/yearsSpan) - 1 fcfCagr = (lastFCF / firstFCF)^(1/yearsSpan) - 1 gap = netIncomeCagr - fcfCagr score = clamp(70 - gap · 250, 0, 100)
gap (net income CAGR − FCF CAGR)Read
≤ -2 ptsFCF outgrowing earnings — conservative, cash-backed
-2 to 2 ptsgrowing in line with each other
2 to 8 ptsearnings outpacing FCF — worth watching
> 8 ptsearnings far outpacing FCF — real divergence

If either CAGR can't be computed because net income or FCF was negative at an endpoint, the model scores 15 when net income still grew (a red flag: earnings up, cash not even positive) rather than silently abstaining; if both signals are unusable it returns insufficient data.

Net income climbing faster than FCF over the same years is the "divergence" this model penalizes.

In the main table, the "Paid in" column shows the distinct months (across the company's full history on record) it has typically paid a dividend in — e.g. "Feb, May, Aug, Nov" for a quarterly payer. Clicking into a company shows every individual payment with its yield at payout: that payment's amount ÷ the average closing price over the 3 months before the payment date, using the company's own real price history — not today's price applied retroactively.

Trailing P/E and EPS remain visible as valuation context in each company's detail view, not as part of any score — a low P/E doesn't predict dividend behavior the way payout ratio and FCF trend do.
