About this prototype

Prototype dashboard · trend recommendations and growth forecasts built from real Yahoo Finance historical data across major global markets.

Not real financial advice. This is a UX prototype. Recommendations are generated by a simple trend-following rule (log-price regression + recent momentum), and forecasts are linear extrapolations of past trends — not predictions. Past performance does not indicate future results.

Forecasting models

Every asset can be analyzed with any of these seven independent models plus an Ensemble that blends them — pick one on the Dashboard's "Forecast models" section to overlay it on the chart, or rank the whole universe by it on the Rankings tab.

Linear Trend

The original model. Fits a linear regression to log-price over the selected lookback and projects it forward — equivalent to extrapolating the average compound growth rate. Also drives the Bullish/Bearish/Neutral recommendation and the composite ranking score, by comparing this long-term trend against recent momentum.

Moving Average Crossover

Compares a short (20-bar) and long (50-bar) simple moving average of log-price. The forecast extrapolates the short average's recent slope — smoother and less sensitive to single-day outliers than the raw linear-trend fit.

ARIMA(1,1,0)

A simplified ARIMA: the log-price series is differenced once, then a first-order autoregression is fit on those differences (no moving-average term, no automatic order selection). A full ARIMA fit needs numerical optimization that's out of scope for a client-side prototype — this is a deliberate, labeled simplification of the standard method.

Holt's Linear Trend

Double exponential smoothing on log-price (a level component and a trend component, no seasonality — irregular trading calendars don't have a clean period to model). The trend is damped (multiplied by a factor below 1 at each step forward) — without damping, a locally strong trend compounds linearly forever and produces wildly overconfident long-horizon forecasts.

GARCH(1,1)

Forecasts volatility, not price direction. Rather than adding its own line to the chart, its volatility estimate sets the width of the Ensemble's uncertainty band, and is shown as a standalone "current annualized volatility" figure.

Feature Regression (ML-style)

A small linear regression predicting next-day return from engineered features — the prior day's return, the gap between a 5-day and 20-day moving average, and a 14-day RSI-like momentum measure — then projected forward recursively, day by day. A genuine feature-engineering + linear-model approach computed fresh in-browser; not a stand-in for a trained Random Forest, LSTM, or Transformer, which don't fit a static client-side prototype.

Sentiment Proxy (simulated)

No real news or text data source is connected. This score is derived purely from a shift in recent price momentum, framed the way a sentiment score would read. It exists to show where a real news/NLP signal would plug into the Ensemble — not as a genuine sentiment measure.

Monte Carlo Simulation

Simulates hundreds of random price paths under geometric Brownian motion, using the asset's historical drift and GARCH-estimated volatility, then reports the 10th-90th percentile of simulated outcomes — a distribution of possible futures instead of one line.

Ensemble

A weighted average of every directional model's forecast (the Sentiment Proxy is weighted at half since it's simulated, not real data). Its uncertainty band comes from GARCH's volatility forecast rather than any single model's own fit.

How Rankings works

The Rankings tab scores every asset in the selected filter (class, country) and lists the top or bottom 1/5/10/25/100 by whichever metric is chosen under "Rank by":

  • Composite score (default) — blends 60% long-term trend growth with 40% recent momentum, then discounts that blend for low-confidence fits (a noisy R²) and for volatility above 20%/yr — so a noisy short-term spike can't outrank a steady, well-supported trend.
  • Any individual model — ranks assets directly by that model's forecast % at the selected lookback. Assets are skipped where the model needs more history than is available at that lookback (e.g. ARIMA, Moving Average Crossover, and Feature Regression all need 30-50+ bars of data).

Use Top to see the highest-scoring assets, or Bottom to see the lowest-scoring ones — useful for spotting names the model is bearish on, not only bullish on. Clicking any row expands its full Dashboard-equivalent detail — chart, recommendation, stats, forecast models, backtest — inline, without leaving the Rankings tab.
